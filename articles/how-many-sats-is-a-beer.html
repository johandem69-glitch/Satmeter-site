Shop versus bar: the gap is the point

Almost four times the price for the same liquid. Nothing about the beer changed — you are paying for rent, staff, licensing and the fact that you did not have to wash a glass.

This makes beer an unusually clean way to see something that gets lost in most price discussions: most of what you pay for most things is not the thing. Converting both numbers into satoshis makes the service premium impossible to ignore, because you are looking at two figures denominated in a unit with no local baggage.

Alcohol pricing is heavily influenced by excise duty and licensing rules, which vary enormously even between neighbouring countries.

You are mostly buying tax

Alcohol carries some of the highest excise duties of any consumer product. In several countries duty and VAT together exceed the cost of production, distribution and retail margin combined.

That gives you a genuinely interesting calculation to run. Take your local beer price, look up your country’s excise duty and VAT rate, and convert just the tax portion into satoshis. Most people find the number startling — and it is a far more visceral way to understand alcohol taxation than any percentage.

Why beer works as an index: it is standardised enough to compare, ubiquitous enough that everyone has a reference price, and taxed differently enough that the variation actually tells you something. The Economist could have used it instead of the Big Mac and lost very little.

The trap in "beers per Bitcoin"

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price and a $1.80 bottle, one Bitcoin is about 55,000 beers. It is a fun number and it circulates widely, but treat it with the suspicion it deserves.

Comparing an appreciated asset against a consumable will always produce a dramatic figure — that is arithmetic, not insight. Run the same calculation on a 1975 house price against beer and you get something equally startling, and nobody presents that as an argument for anything.

The honest caveat. Every "cheaper in sats over time" claim depends entirely on the start date you pick. Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches everything got dramatically more expensive in satoshis. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a beer?

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price a $1.80 supermarket bottle is about 1,800 satoshis, and a $7.00 bar pint about 7,000 satoshis.

Why is bar beer nearly four times shop beer?

Rent, staff, licensing and service. It is a clear illustration that most of what you pay for most things is not the thing itself.

How much of a beer price is tax?

In several countries excise duty and VAT together exceed production, distribution and retail margin combined. Rates vary enormously even between neighbouring countries.

How many beers can one Bitcoin buy?

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price and a $1.80 bottle, roughly 55,000. Treat this framing cautiously — comparing an appreciated asset to a consumable always flatters the asset.
