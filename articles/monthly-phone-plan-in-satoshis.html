India versus the United States: a genuine puzzle

An Indian plan costs a small fraction of an American one in satoshi terms, and it is typically a better plan — more data, often unlimited calls. This is not a rounding error or a subsidy artefact; it is one of the largest price gaps for a comparable service anywhere in the world economy.

The explanation is a mix of aggressive price competition, enormous subscriber scale spreading network costs thin, lower infrastructure and labour costs, and a regulatory environment that tolerated brutal price wars. The American market is more consolidated, and consolidated markets do not compete on price.

This is worth sitting with, because it complicates a story people like to tell. If you priced the same plan in satoshis in 2015 and today, the Indian figure fell for reasons that have almost nothing to do with Bitcoin and everything to do with a telecoms price war. Sats-denominated prices absorb every real-world change, not just monetary ones.

The number actually worth knowing: sats per gigabyte. Divide your monthly cost in sats by your data allowance. It turns an invisible subscription into a unit price you can compare against any other plan — and most people discover they are paying for far more data than they use.

The audit that pays for itself

Mobile plans are where the "convert your bills to sats" exercise is most likely to save you real money, for a boring reason: you probably signed up years ago and the market moved.

Add up twelve months. Convert to satoshis. Then check what the same carrier charges a new customer today for the same allowance. In competitive markets the gap is frequently substantial, and the sats figure has a way of turning "I should look into that" into an actual phone call.

The honest caveat. Every "cheaper in sats over time" claim depends entirely on the start date you pick. Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches everything got dramatically more expensive in satoshis. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a monthly phone plan?

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price a $30 plan is about 30,000 satoshis a month, or roughly 360,000 satoshis a year.

Why are Indian mobile plans so much cheaper than American ones?

Aggressive price competition, enormous subscriber scale, lower infrastructure and labour costs, and a regulator that tolerated price wars. The US market is more consolidated, and consolidated markets compete less on price.

What is sats per gigabyte?

Your monthly plan cost in satoshis divided by your data allowance. It converts an invisible subscription into a comparable unit price.

Does a falling sats price for phone plans prove anything about Bitcoin?

Not on its own. Indian mobile prices fell largely because of a telecoms price war. Sats-denominated prices absorb every real-world change, not only monetary ones.
