Gallons, litres, and the trap in between

Most of the world sells fuel by the litre; the United States sells it by the gallon. A US gallon is 3.785 litres, so any cross-border comparison that skips this conversion is off by nearly a factor of four. This is the single most common error in international fuel comparisons.

The spread here is enormous — a gallon in the Netherlands costs several times what it costs in the United States. Almost none of that is the oil. It is tax policy, and in some countries it is subsidy policy running the other way.

Why fuel is the best inflation tell

Economists often strip energy out of inflation figures because it is volatile. For a household that is precisely backwards: energy is the price that tells you something is happening before the official statistics do.

Pricing fuel in satoshis stacks two volatile things on top of each other, which sounds like a mess but is actually informative. You are watching an energy market and a monetary experiment interact, and the two do not move together.

The energy connection people miss: Bitcoin mining consumes electricity, and mining economics are partly driven by energy prices. So fuel and Bitcoin are not fully independent variables. The link is indirect and much weaker than "Bitcoin is backed by energy" arguments suggest — but it is not zero, and honest analysis should mention it.

How much of your tank is tax?

In much of Europe, roughly half of the pump price is duty and VAT. So when you convert a Dutch or British fill-up into satoshis, about half the resulting number represents government revenue rather than fuel.

That is a genuinely interesting thing to look at directly. Take your pump price, apply your country’s fuel duty and VAT rates, and convert just the tax portion into satoshis. Most people are surprised by how large that number is — and it is a far more concrete way to think about tax than a percentage.

The honest caveat. A falling sats price is often presented as proof that Bitcoin "always" gains purchasing power. That framing quietly hides every period where the opposite happened — and Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions. During those stretches everything on this page got dramatically more expensive in satoshis. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a gallon of gas?

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price a $3.20 gallon is about 3,200 satoshis. The live figure above is computed from the current Bitcoin price.

How do I compare litre prices to gallon prices?

Multiply the litre price by 3.785 to get the US gallon equivalent. Skipping this conversion is the most common error in international fuel comparisons and makes numbers wrong by almost four times.

Why is fuel so much more expensive in Europe than the United States?

Almost entirely tax. Fuel duty and VAT make up roughly half the pump price in much of Europe, while some other countries subsidise fuel in the opposite direction.

Are Bitcoin and energy prices connected?

Indirectly. Bitcoin mining consumes electricity, so mining economics respond to energy costs. The link is far weaker than 'Bitcoin is backed by energy' claims suggest, but it is not zero.
