Groceries in Bitcoin: Vietnam

Fourth in the world for crypto adoption, without a currency crisis to explain it.

Live figures · recalculated … from … · by Johan Demmers

Every other country in this series has an obvious reason to reach for Bitcoin: the currency is collapsing, the banks failed, or the state is printing. Vietnam has none of those, and still ranked fourth in the world in the 2025 Chainalysis Global Crypto Adoption Index. That makes it the most interesting page here, because it breaks the standard explanation.

The currency that did not collapse

The dong traded near 23,000 to the dollar in September 2021 and near 26,124 in August 2026. That is a depreciation of roughly 14% over five years, and something like 16% over ten. Compare that with Pakistan’s 69% over the same five-year window, or Argentina and Venezuela, where the arithmetic needs scientific notation.

Inflation was 4.45% year on year in July 2026. There is a gap between the official and free-market dollar rate, which reached nearly 1,500 dong, around 5%, in late October 2025 and was described as the widest in twelve years, driven by dollar shortages and gold arbitrage. But a 5% gap is a wrinkle, not a crisis.

So the usual explanation does not work here. Vietnamese people are not buying crypto because the dong is destroying their savings. Something else is going on.

What is actually going on

Two things, mostly. The first is capital controls. Vietnam maintains strict rules on moving money out, and there is a long-standing cultural habit of holding value in dollars or gold rather than dong. Dollar-pegged stablecoins slot straight into that habit and route around the controls.

The second is cross-border work and remittances. Vietnam received an estimated $16 billion in remittances in 2025, with Ho Chi Minh City alone accounting for around $10.5 billion. The World Bank puts the cost of sending $200 from the United States to Vietnam at 5.27% in the third quarter of 2025, meaningfully worse than the Pakistan corridor. Freelancers and remote workers being paid in USDT rather than through banks is now ordinary, and the informal over-the-counter market runs through messaging apps and café agents.

Note what almost none of that is about: Bitcoin as a store of value. Most of the activity is stablecoins used as dollars. If you want the honest version of Vietnam’s fourth-place ranking, it is that Vietnam is very good at using crypto rails to hold and move dollars.

Legal to own, illegal to spend

Vietnam spent years in a grey zone and started closing it in 2025. The Law on Digital Technology Industry, passed in June 2025 and effective 1 January 2026, explicitly recognises digital assets as property. So owning crypto is lawful and it is treated as an asset class.

Paying with it is not. All transactions must settle in dong, and stablecoins are specifically barred from use as a payment method. A five-year pilot for licensed virtual asset service providers was adopted in September 2025, but only tokenised real-world assets issued by Vietnamese enterprises may trade on it, and reportedly only five licences will be issued for the entire pilot.

That is close to the opposite of South Africa, where you can pay for groceries in bitcoin at 1,500 supermarkets. In Vietnam you may legally hold it and may not legally spend it.

Hours of work, not just sats

Vietnam sets its minimum wage by region. From 1 January 2026 it runs from 3,700,000 dong a month in Region 4 to 5,310,000 in Region 1, which covers Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. At the top rate that is about $203 a month.

The eight-item basket above totals roughly 409,000 dong, or about $15.65 — close to 8% of a Region 1 minimum monthly wage. That is a better ratio than Pakistan and a far better one than Venezuela, and it is the number that actually describes daily life.

Sats per basket tells you about Bitcoin. Baskets per paycheque tells you about Vietnam.

The honest caveat. Comparing a depreciating currency against Bitcoin over a period when Bitcoin rose will always favour Bitcoin. Bitcoin has also fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches it was the worse place to have been. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a Vietnamese grocery basket?

The eight-item basket totals roughly 409,000 dong, or about $15.65 at 26,124.5 dong to the dollar. The live table converts that dollar figure into satoshis at the current Bitcoin price.

Why does Vietnam rank so high for crypto adoption?

Not because of inflation. The dong has been relatively stable. The drivers are strict capital controls, a cultural preference for holding dollars or gold, and large remittance and freelance-payment flows where formal transfer costs run above 5%.

Can you pay with Bitcoin in Vietnam?

No. All transactions must settle in dong and stablecoins are barred as a payment method. Crypto is legally recognised as property and as an asset class under the Law on Digital Technology Industry, effective 1 January 2026, but it is not legal to spend.

How much has the Vietnamese dong depreciated?

Roughly 14% against the dollar over five years and about 16% over ten, far less than most other countries in this series.
