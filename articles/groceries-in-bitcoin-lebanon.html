Groceries in Bitcoin: Lebanon

The country where the banks failed before the currency did — and why that is a different argument entirely.

Live figures · recalculated 3 September 2026 from Blockchain.info · by Johan Demmers · Published 23 August 2026 · updated 3 September 2026

Every other country in this series tells a story about inflation. Lebanon tells a story about counterparties, and it is the more uncomfortable one. In October 2019 the Lebanese banking system stopped letting depositors withdraw their own dollars. Roughly four million people learned that a balance on a screen and money you can actually spend are two different things.

Prices here are in US dollars because that is how Lebanese supermarkets have priced goods since March 2023. The pound still exists and still circulates, repegged at about 89,500 to the dollar since December 2023 and sitting near 89,600 in August 2026. But the shelf tag is a dollar tag.

What a banking collapse looks like from inside

The early official estimate of losses across the Lebanese financial system was around $70 billion. The World Bank described the model that produced them as Ponzi finance. More recent figures put the gap between what depositors are owed and what the banks actually hold at $80 to $83 billion.

Depositors have not been made whole and mostly have not been paid. Two central bank circulars, 158 and 166, created a partial escape valve: $800 a month for some depositors, $400 for others. By the end of 2024, 431,448 depositors had between them extracted about $3.24 billion through those channels since they launched. Against an $80 billion hole, that is a rounding error.

A bank restructuring law passed Parliament on 31 July 2025, but it cannot take effect until a separate financial-gap law is ratified. That bill was approved by cabinet on 27 December 2025 and sent to Parliament. I could not find confirmation that it has passed. Treat Lebanese depositor compensation as unresolved, because it is.

The pound: from 1,507.5 to 89,500

The Lebanese pound was pegged at 1,507.5 to the dollar from 1997. The peg did not survive the crisis. The parallel rate hit LL111,000 to the dollar on 18 March 2023, its worst ever level. In December 2023 the central bank gave up defending the fiction and set the official rate at LL89,500, roughly where the market already was.

It has held there since, which is genuinely notable. Inflation ran between 12% and 20% year on year through the first half of 2026, high but nothing like the peak crisis years. The currency, in other words, has stabilised. The banking system has not.

Why this is the strongest version of the self-custody argument

Bitcoin’s usual sales pitch is about inflation, and in most countries that pitch competes with simply holding dollars. In Lebanon it does not, because the problem was never that dollars lost value. The problem was that dollars in a Lebanese bank stopped being dollars you could withdraw. They got a nickname, lollars, and a haircut.

That is precisely the failure mode an asset you hold yourself is designed to avoid. If you want the clearest real-world case for self-custody, it is here, not in the countries with the highest inflation rates.

Two honest qualifications. First, most Lebanese crypto use is not Bitcoin at all — reporting on the local market puts around 80% of trading volume in USDT, a dollar-pegged token with its own issuer and its own counterparty risk. People wanted dollars they could hold, not sound money theory. Second, the Banque du Liban barred banks from handling crypto in 2013 and the Capital Markets Authority extended that to licensed financial institutions in 2018, so this all runs through informal peer-to-peer brokers with no consumer protection at all. The traditional US-to-Lebanon remittance corridor averaged $18.71 in fees per $200 sent; the crypto route undercuts that and offers you nothing if it goes wrong.

Hours of work, not just sats

Lebanon’s minimum wage was raised to 28 million pounds a month in June 2025, effective that July, which is about $313. That is a 56% increase on the previous level and still less than half the pre-crisis minimum of roughly $450.

The eight-item basket above totals about $35, so a minimum-wage month buys roughly nine of them before rent, transport or electricity. The World Bank’s most recent assessment, using 2022 data, found poverty had more than tripled in a decade to 44% of the population.

Sats per basket tells you about Bitcoin. Baskets per paycheque tells you about Lebanon.

The honest caveat. Comparing a depreciating currency against Bitcoin over a period when Bitcoin rose will always favour Bitcoin. Bitcoin has also fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches it was the worse place to have been. Sources and method

How the satoshi figures are calculated. Nothing in the satoshi column is typed in by hand. Each one is the local price divided by Bitcoin’s current price in US dollars and multiplied by 100,000,000, because one bitcoin is one hundred million satoshis — the smallest unit defined in the Bitcoin white paper. The exchange rate is fetched from Blockchain.info and refreshed every sixty seconds, with Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp as fallbacks. If your browser cannot reach any of them, the figures you see are the ones calculated on 3 September 2026, when one bitcoin was $77,772.39.

Where the local prices come from. The prices in the basket are indicative national averages reviewed on 3 September 2026, not one supermarket’s shelf on one day. They vary widely by city, by shop and by season, and they are cross-checked against Numbeo's cost-of-living data for Lebanon, which is crowd-sourced and carries that same caveat. The calculator above exists precisely so you can replace them with the prices you actually pay.

Where the currency and inflation context comes from. Statements on this page about inflation, devaluation and the direction of the local currency are checked against World Bank and IMF consumer-price data for Lebanon. Figures about Bitcoin adoption are order-of-magnitude estimates from published survey and chain-analysis work, and are described as such in the text rather than quoted as precise percentages.

A note on the currency used here. Prices are given in US dollars because Lebanese supermarkets have priced in dollars since March 2023. That is the shelf price, not a conversion.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a Lebanese grocery basket?

The eight-item basket on this page totals about $35 at the dollar prices Beirut supermarkets quote. The live table converts that into satoshis at the current Bitcoin price.

Why does this page price Lebanon in dollars?

Because Lebanese supermarkets have priced goods in US dollars since March 2023. The pound still circulates and has been pegged near 89,500 to the dollar since December 2023, but the shelf price is a dollar price.

What are lollars?

An informal term for US dollar balances trapped inside Lebanese banks after October 2019. They can only be withdrawn under restrictive central bank circulars, at administrative rates well below what the deposit was originally worth.

Have Lebanese depositors been compensated?

Not yet. A bank restructuring law passed in July 2025 but cannot take effect until a separate financial-gap law is ratified. That bill went to Parliament in late December 2025 and no confirmation of its passage was found.
