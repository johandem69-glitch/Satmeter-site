Why hold the dollar prices fixed?

This is the design decision that makes the index useful, and it is worth explaining because it looks like laziness at first glance.

If both the basket contents and the dollar prices changed every month, the index would measure two things at once and tell you nothing clean about either. By pinning the reference prices, every movement in the sats total comes from one source: the Bitcoin exchange rate. That is a single-variable measurement, which is the only kind you can reason about.

The trade-off is real and worth stating plainly: this index does not track grocery inflation. A basket that costs $113 today will genuinely cost more in a few years, and this page will not show that. It tracks how much Bitcoin you need to hand over for a fixed quantity of ordinary life.

The shortcut worth memorising: at a Bitcoin price of $100,000, 1,000 sats = $1. So a $113 basket is roughly 113,000 sats. Once that anchor is in your head, you can convert almost any everyday price without a calculator.

How to read the index over time

One snapshot is trivia. The series is the interesting object, and it behaves in a way that surprises people the first time:

  • Bitcoin rises → the index falls. Fewer sats buy the same shopping. This is the direction Bitcoin holders quote.
  • Bitcoin falls → the index rises. The same shopping costs more sats. This direction gets quoted far less often, which should tell you something about how these charts are usually presented.
  • Bitcoin flat → the index is flat. Because the dollar prices are pinned, the index simply does not move on its own.

If you want a genuinely useful record, note the number once a month on the same day and write the date beside it. Twelve entries and you have something more honest than most Bitcoin commentary: your own series, unfiltered by whoever chose the start date.

The honest caveat. A falling sats price is often presented as proof that Bitcoin "always" gains purchasing power. That framing quietly hides every period where the opposite happened — and Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions. During those stretches everything on this page got dramatically more expensive in satoshis. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Using this as a citable figure

A single number with a name travels much further than an opinion. "The Sats Grocery Index is at roughly 113,000 sats" is quotable in a way that "Bitcoin is up" is not, and journalists and newsletter writers reach for figures they can attribute.

If you quote it, please include the date and the Bitcoin price you saw, because without those two things the number is meaningless a week later. That is not a formality — it is the difference between a statistic and a vibe.

Sources and method

How the satoshi figures are calculated. Nothing in the satoshi column is typed in by hand. Each one is the local price divided by Bitcoin’s current price in US dollars and multiplied by 100,000,000, because one bitcoin is one hundred million satoshis — the smallest unit defined in the Bitcoin white paper. The exchange rate is fetched from Blockchain.info and refreshed every sixty seconds, with Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp as fallbacks. If your browser cannot reach any of them, the figures you see are the ones calculated on 3 September 2026, when one bitcoin was $77,772.39.

Where the prices come from. The prices used here are indicative averages reviewed on 3 September 2026, cross-checked against Numbeo's cost-of-living database and, for United States figures, against the US Bureau of Labor Statistics consumer price index. They are not a single retailer’s prices and they move. The converter on the home page lets you substitute the price you actually pay, which is the only number that means anything for your own budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Sats Grocery Index?

A live figure showing what a fixed basket of fourteen everyday items and monthly bills costs in satoshis. The dollar reference prices are held stable so that every movement in the index comes from Bitcoin's exchange rate rather than from changing shopping habits.

Why does the index fall when Bitcoin rises?

Because fewer satoshis are needed to buy the same goods. The shopping did not get cheaper; each satoshi became more valuable in dollar terms.

Does this index track grocery inflation?

No, and that is deliberate. The dollar reference prices are pinned, so the index isolates Bitcoin's exchange rate. For real grocery inflation you would need a basket whose local prices are re-surveyed over time.

How often does the index update?

Every time you load the page, and then automatically about once a minute. It is computed from a live Bitcoin price rather than stored, so there is no stale figure to go out of date.
