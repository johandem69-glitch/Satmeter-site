Groceries in Bitcoin: India

The cheapest basket here — and the clearest lesson in why cheap in sats isn't the same as affordable.

Live figures · recalculated … from … · by Johan Demmers

India is the largest market in this collection by population and one of the most interesting, because its story is not currency collapse. The rupee has depreciated gradually against the dollar over decades rather than dramatically, and Indian inflation has generally been managed.

What makes India compelling instead is scale, youth and regulatory ambiguity — and a food basket that is genuinely cheap in global terms.

The cheapest basket here, and what that reveals

The Indian basket above totals under ₹1,000, which converts to a satoshi figure far below the American or Dutch equivalents. It is tempting to read that as India being inexpensive, and in absolute terms it is. But the reading is incomplete in a way that matters.

Food costs less in India partly because of enormous domestic agricultural production and long-standing subsidy programmes, and partly because wages are much lower. A basket that costs a fifth as many satoshis as the American one does not mean Indians experience it as five times cheaper — measured as a share of income, food takes up a considerably larger portion of an Indian household budget than an American one.

This is the clearest illustration on this site of why sats-per-basket and affordability are different questions. Denominating in a global unit strips out the exchange rate but it also strips out income, and income is most of what determines whether a price is affordable. A satoshi figure alone will consistently make poorer countries look cheap, which is true for a visitor and misleading about the residents.

The tax regime that shapes behaviour

India also has the most consequential regulatory situation of any market here. Crypto is not banned, but it sits under a tax regime that is heavy by international standards — a flat tax on gains with no offsetting of losses, plus a transaction-level deduction. The practical effect is that active trading is expensive and long-term holding is comparatively favoured.

That is worth understanding before drawing conclusions from any Indian adoption figure. High reported ownership alongside a punitive trading tax describes a different behaviour pattern than the same ownership figure in a light-touch jurisdiction. And a good deal of Indian activity has moved to offshore venues, which makes the official numbers an unreliable guide to what is actually happening.

None of which is tax advice — rules change and the details matter enormously. If you are in India and considering anything beyond reading this page, the tax treatment is the first thing to establish, not the last, and it is worth paying a professional to get right.

Hours of work, not just sats

The sats figure tells you about Bitcoin. It does not tell you whether life in India is affordable. For that, the useful denominator is time.

Indian median individual income is modest relative to headline GDP figures, and food remains a large share of household spending for most of the population. Divide that by the basket total and you get the number that actually matters to a household: how much of a working week disappears into groceries. That figure is comparable across borders in a way that neither local prices nor satoshi prices are.

Run both calculations and you learn two different things. Sats per basket tells you about the monetary side; hours per basket tells you about the living-standards side. Confusing the two is the most common mistake in this entire genre of analysis, and it is usually made on purpose.

The honest caveat. Comparing a depreciating currency against Bitcoin over a period when Bitcoin rose will always favour Bitcoin. Bitcoin has also fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches it was the worse place to have been. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is an Indian grocery basket?

The eight-item basket above totals roughly ₹946. The live satoshi figure is computed from Bitcoin's current rupee price.

Why is the Indian basket so much cheaper in satoshis?

Large domestic agricultural production, subsidy programmes and much lower wages. Measured as a share of household income, however, food takes up a larger portion in India than in the United States.

Does a low satoshi price mean India is affordable?

Not necessarily. Satoshi figures strip out the exchange rate but also strip out income, which is most of what determines affordability. Poorer countries will always look cheap in this measure.

How is crypto taxed in India?

Under a regime that is heavy by international standards, including a flat tax on gains without loss offsetting plus a transaction-level deduction. Rules change, so verify current treatment with a qualified professional.
