Groceries in Bitcoin: Venezuela

Fourteen zeros gone since 2008, and a food basket that costs three times the minimum income.

Live figures · recalculated … from … · by Johan Demmers

Venezuela is the country people reach for when they want to make Bitcoin’s case in a single word, and it is the country where that case needs the most care. The bolívar has shed fourteen zeros across three redenominations since 2008. Annual inflation was running near 580% in July 2026 according to the central bank’s own figures, which reporters routinely treat with caution.

The prices below are in US dollars, and that is not a shortcut — but it is not the whole picture either. Venezuelan shops widely reference dollar prices, yet an Ecoanalítica study of roughly 49,000 transactions found about three quarters were still settled in bolívares in December 2024, and that share had climbed above 80% by May 2025. Quoted in dollars, paid in bolívares, at whichever rate applies that day. That is the honest description.

Fourteen zeros

Venezuela has redenominated its currency three times in seventeen years. The bolívar fuerte removed three zeros in 2008. The bolívar soberano removed five more in 2018. The digital bolívar removed another six in 2021. Fourteen zeros in total, which means one of today’s bolívares represents one hundred trillion of the pre-2008 units.

Redenomination does not fix anything. It relabels. Each time, the arithmetic got easier and the underlying problem did not move.

Two exchange rates, and the gap between them

The central bank rate sat around 784.66 bolívares to the dollar on 21 August 2026. The Binance peer-to-peer rate, which is what most people can actually transact at, was around 920.78 on 23 August. That is a gap of roughly 15%, and it moves.

Over the twelve months to August 2026 the bolívar weakened by something on the order of 450% against the dollar. This is why the bolívar figure is not used anywhere on this page: there is no single number that is true for everyone, and picking one would be a decision disguised as a measurement.

The number that actually describes Venezuela

Forget satoshis for a moment. CENDAS-FVM prices a basic family food basket every month. In July 2026 it came to $727.89, or $741.41 once drinking water is included.

The total minimum income, base salary plus the government bonus programmes, rose to $240 a month on 1 May 2026. The legal base minimum salary itself is still 130 bolívares, frozen since March 2022 and now worth small change — which matters, because pensions and severance are calculated from that frozen base rather than from the bonuses.

So the basic food basket for a family costs roughly three times the total minimum income. No denomination in satoshis, dollars or bolívares changes that ratio. It is the single most useful figure on this page.

What Venezuelans actually hold

Venezuela ranked 18th in the 2025 Chainalysis Global Crypto Adoption Index, and 9th once adjusted for population. That is high per-capita activity by any measure, and it fits the circumstances.

Two things complicate the tidy narrative. First, the state’s own cryptocurrency, the Petro, was declared dead in January 2024 after six years of going nowhere — a reminder that a government issuing a token is not the same category of thing as Bitcoin. Second, mining has been repeatedly crushed rather than encouraged: large operations were shut down from March 2023 after the SUNACRIP corruption scandal, more than 2,300 mining units were seized in Maracay in May 2024, and the government moved to disconnect miners from the grid that same month.

Hours of work, not just sats

The eight-item basket above totals about $36. Against a total minimum income of $240 a month, one basket is roughly 15% of everything a minimum-income household receives.

That is the comparison worth carrying across borders. Sats per basket tells you about Bitcoin. Baskets per paycheque tells you about the country.

The honest caveat. Comparing a depreciating currency against Bitcoin over a period when Bitcoin rose will always favour Bitcoin. Bitcoin has also fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches it was the worse place to have been. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a Venezuelan grocery basket?

The eight-item basket on this page totals about $36 at the dollar prices Venezuelan shops quote. The live table converts that into satoshis at the current Bitcoin price.

Why does this page use dollars instead of bolivares?

Because there is no single bolivar exchange rate that is true for everyone. The central bank rate and the peer-to-peer rate differed by roughly 15% in August 2026, so any bolivar figure would be a choice dressed up as a measurement. Venezuelan shops also widely quote prices in dollars.

Is Venezuela actually dollarized?

Not formally, and not completely. Prices are widely quoted in dollars, but an Ecoanalitica study found around three quarters of transactions were still settled in bolivares in December 2024, rising above 80% by May 2025.

How many zeros has the bolivar lost?

Fourteen, across three redenominations: three zeros in 2008, five in 2018 and six in 2021.
