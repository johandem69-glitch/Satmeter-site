The compounding question

Here is where subscriptions get genuinely interesting, and where honest framing matters most.

A monthly subscription in sats invites an obvious thought: what if I had kept those sats instead? Twelve payments a year, every year, in an asset that has historically appreciated. The arithmetic looks dramatic.

Be careful with it, though, for two reasons that get skipped in almost every version of this argument:

  • It assumes the past repeats. Backward-looking compounding on any asset that has already appreciated will always look spectacular. That is a property of the arithmetic, not evidence about the future.
  • It prices the alternative at zero. You did not spend nothing — you watched things. Framing consumption as pure loss is the same reasoning that concludes you should never eat at a restaurant.

The defensible version of the exercise is narrower and more useful: it makes recurring costs visible. Most people underestimate their subscription total by a wide margin, and seeing it as one annual sats figure is a genuinely effective way to notice.

Try this instead: add up every subscription you actually pay — streaming, music, cloud storage, apps, gym, the thing you forgot — and convert the annual total. Most people land somewhere north of a thousand dollars. As a single sats number, that tends to prompt a decision rather than a shrug.

Why "sats per hour watched" beats "sats per month"

A subscription you use daily and one you forgot to cancel cost the same in satoshis but are not remotely the same purchase. Divide your monthly sats figure by hours actually watched and the number becomes a decision tool rather than a talking point.

At $17.99 and twenty hours a month, you are paying roughly 900 sats an hour at a $100,000 Bitcoin price. At two hours a month, it is nine thousand. Same bill, radically different value — and only one of those two numbers tells you whether to keep it.

The honest caveat. A falling sats price is often presented as proof that Bitcoin "always" gains purchasing power. That framing quietly hides every period where the opposite happened — and Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions. During those stretches everything on this page got dramatically more expensive in satoshis. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a Netflix subscription?

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price a $17.99 plan is about 17,990 satoshis a month, or roughly 215,900 satoshis a year. The live figure above uses the current price.

Why is Netflix so much cheaper in India in sats terms?

Because Netflix prices regionally by market, not globally. Denominating in a single global unit like satoshis makes the size of that gap unusually visible.

Should I cancel subscriptions and buy Bitcoin instead?

This page does not give advice. The backward-looking compounding argument always flatters an asset that has already appreciated, and it prices your actual enjoyment at zero. Useful for noticing recurring costs; not a basis for a financial decision.

What is a better way to judge a subscription in sats?

Divide the monthly sats figure by the hours you actually use it. Sats per hour turns the number into a decision tool rather than a talking point.
