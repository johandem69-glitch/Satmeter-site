How Many Sats Is a Cup of Coffee?

The most useful Bitcoin yardstick has nothing to do with coffee — and everything to do with buying one often enough to remember the price.

By Johan Demmers · Updated 12 August 2026 · 6 min read

A cup of coffee is the most useful Bitcoin yardstick there is, and it has nothing to do with coffee. It is useful because you buy one often enough to remember what it costs, and because almost everyone on earth has a reference price in their head.

So: how many satoshis is a coffee today?

The short answer

At a Bitcoin price of $100,000, a $4.00 café coffee costs 4,000 satoshis. Convenient, and worth memorising as an anchor: at a $100,000 Bitcoin, one dollar is 1,000 sats. Every other calculation flows from that.

If Bitcoin sits at $50,000, the same coffee is 8,000 sats. At $200,000 it is 2,000 sats. The coffee did not change — the measuring stick did.

A Copenhagen espresso costs roughly five times as many satoshis as a Lisbon one. That gap is not about Bitcoin at all; it is wages, rent and taxes. Bitcoin simply makes the comparison unusually clean, because there is one Bitcoin price rather than dozens of exchange rates to argue about.

Why coffee beats a stock chart as a mental model

Most people meet Bitcoin as a price chart, which trains you to think of it as a bet. Pricing a coffee in sats trains something different: thinking of Bitcoin as a unit of account.

Those are genuinely different mental modes. A chart asks "will this go up?". A sats price asks "what does my money buy?". The second question is the one that has occupied economists for centuries, and it is the more interesting one.

A quick anchor worth remembering: at a $100,000 Bitcoin price, 1,000 sats = $1. So a 4,000-sat coffee, a 2,500-sat loaf of bread and a 30,000-sat phone bill are all instantly readable once you hold that one number in your head.

The "Bitcoin pizza" problem, and what it really teaches

Every Bitcoin conversation eventually reaches Laszlo Hanyecz, who in 2010 paid 10,000 BTC for two pizzas. At today's prices that is an eye-watering sum, and the story is usually told as a cautionary tale about spending Bitcoin.

That reading is too simple. In 2010 Bitcoin had no established market price, no exchanges worth the name, and no certainty it would exist in a year. Hanyecz demonstrated that the thing worked as money — which is arguably why it went on to be worth anything at all.

The genuinely useful lesson is narrower: denominating things in sats makes long-term changes visible in a way fiat prices hide. A pizza going from 5,000,000,000 sats to roughly 20,000 sats is a fact about Bitcoin's history. It is not a prediction about its future, and treating it as one is how people talk themselves into decisions they regret.

Worth being blunt about: the "your coffee keeps getting cheaper in sats" argument only holds over periods when Bitcoin rose. Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions. During those stretches your coffee got much more expensive in sats. Anyone showing you only the flattering direction is selling something. Nothing here is financial advice.

How to build the habit

If you want thinking in sats to actually stick, three things help:

  • Pick one purchase you make weekly. Coffee, bread, a commute ticket. One is enough — variety defeats the purpose.
  • Write the number down with the date. A note on your phone works. Without the date it is worthless later.
  • Check monthly, not hourly. Daily noise tells you nothing and mostly just triggers anxiety.

After six months you will have something more informative than most Bitcoin commentary: your own series, in your own currency, for something you actually buy.

Frequently asked questions

How many sats is a cup of coffee?

At a $100,000 Bitcoin price, a $4.00 coffee is about 4,000 satoshis. Use the calculator above with your own local price for an exact live figure.

How many sats is one dollar?

It depends entirely on Bitcoin's price. At $100,000 per Bitcoin, one dollar is 1,000 satoshis. At $50,000 it is 2,000 satoshis.

Why do coffee prices in sats differ between countries?

Because the coffee itself costs different amounts locally. Bitcoin has a single global price, so every difference in the sats figure comes from local wages, rent, taxes and supply chains.

Should I actually pay for coffee with Bitcoin?

That is a personal decision with tax consequences in many countries, since spending Bitcoin can be a taxable disposal. This page is an educational tool, not advice — check your local rules or ask a qualified professional.
