Groceries in Bitcoin: South Africa

The only country here where you can actually pay for the basket in bitcoin at the till.

Live figures · recalculated … from … · by Johan Demmers

Every other page in this series is a thought experiment. South Africa is not. Pick n Pay began piloting Bitcoin Lightning payments in 39 stores in November 2022 and took it nationwide in February 2023, across somewhere between 1,500 and 1,628 stores depending on which report you read. You can scan a QR code at the till and walk out with the basket.

It is also, like Brazil, a country where the local-currency figure needs no apology. The rand trades in deep, market-determined markets, so the satoshi figures below are a direct calculation rather than an approximation of one.

How paying with bitcoin at a till actually works

The technology behind it is CryptoConvert’s CryptoQR system, connected to the store terminals through Electrum. At the till you choose the contactless QR option, scan the code with a Lightning wallet, approve it, and the slip prints within seconds. Compatible wallets and exchanges include Luno, VALR, Binance, Blink, Phoenix, Strike and Wallet of Satoshi, among others.

One caveat worth stating rather than glossing over. Every description of the system says the bitcoin is converted at the moment of payment, which means this is Bitcoin working as a payment rail rather than Pick n Pay choosing to hold bitcoin. I could not find an explicit statement of how the retailer itself is settled. If someone tells you a major South African grocery chain holds bitcoin on its balance sheet, ask them for the source.

That distinction matters less than it sounds. For the person in the queue, the useful question is whether sats spend, and here they do.

Food inflation just hit a sixteen-year low, and it did not help enough

Statistics South Africa reported headline inflation of 4.3% year on year in July 2026, down from 5.0% in June. Food and non-alcoholic beverage inflation came in at 0.9%, the lowest reading in more than sixteen years, since June 2010. Petrol fell 7.1% and diesel 11.7% between June and July.

Now set that against the Pietermaritzburg Economic Justice and Dignity Group’s household food basket, which cost R5,530.52 in July 2026. The national minimum wage from 1 March 2026 is R30.23 an hour, or about R5,562.32 a month. The food basket costs almost exactly one full minimum wage, before transport, before electricity, before rent.

That is the point people miss when they cheer a low inflation print. Inflation measures the rate of change. Affordability is a level. Prices can stop rising and still be out of reach, and in South Africa they are: unemployment was 33.6% in the second quarter of 2026.

A rand that had a good year

The rand traded around R16.00 to the dollar on 21 August 2026, roughly 8.2% stronger than twelve months earlier, after touching an all-time low near R19.93 in April 2025.

This is worth flagging because it cuts against the script. The standard emerging-market Bitcoin argument assumes the local currency is in freefall. In the twelve months to August 2026 the rand was not. If you are looking for a country where a naive sats-versus-fiat chart would flatter Bitcoin least, South Africa in this particular window is a decent candidate. Publishing the awkward year alongside the convenient ones is the whole reason these pages are worth reading.

Regulation caught up

The Financial Sector Conduct Authority began licensing crypto asset service providers on 1 June 2023. By January 2026 it had received 512 applications, granted 300 licences, declined 14 and seen 121 withdrawn, and had opened 81 investigations into unlicensed operators. Crypto assets are legally classified as financial products under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act.

A Discovery Bank report published in May 2026 estimated that around 7.8 million South Africans used cryptocurrency platforms during the first half of 2025, though other surveys give notably lower numbers and the methodologies differ. Separately, the Reserve Bank has been tightening the rules on moving crypto across the border under exchange control, so the offshore side is getting less relaxed rather than more.

Hours of work, not just sats

The eight-item basket above totals about R350. At the national minimum wage of R30.23 an hour, that is roughly eleven and a half hours of work.

Sats per basket tells you about Bitcoin. Hours per basket tells you about South Africa. Both numbers are on this page; only one of them is affected by what Bitcoin did last week.

The honest caveat. Comparing a depreciating currency against Bitcoin over a period when Bitcoin rose will always favour Bitcoin. Bitcoin has also fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches it was the worse place to have been. Nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here is financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a South African grocery basket?

The eight-item basket on this page totals about R350. The live table converts that into satoshis using the current rand Bitcoin price, which is a direct calculation rather than a proxy.

Can you really pay with Bitcoin at Pick n Pay?

Yes. Pick n Pay piloted Bitcoin Lightning payments in 39 stores in November 2022 and rolled it out nationwide in February 2023 across roughly 1,500 to 1,628 stores, using CryptoConvert's QR system connected through Electrum.

Is the rand a reliable currency for this calculation?

Yes. The rand trades in deep, market-determined markets with no meaningful local Bitcoin premium to correct for, so the South African figures are as direct as the US or Brazilian ones.

Is crypto legal and regulated in South Africa?

Yes. Crypto assets are classified as financial products, and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority has licensed crypto asset service providers since June 2023, granting 300 licences out of 512 applications by January 2026.
