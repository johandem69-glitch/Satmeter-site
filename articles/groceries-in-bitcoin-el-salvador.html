Groceries in Bitcoin: El Salvador

The only country that made Bitcoin legal tender, and the only country that changed its mind.

Live figures · recalculated 3 September 2026 from Blockchain.info · by Johan Demmers · Published 23 August 2026 · updated 3 September 2026

El Salvador is the only country that ever made Bitcoin legal tender. It is also the only country that ever reversed that decision. Almost everything written about El Salvador tells you the first half of that sentence and stops, which is how a page ends up out of date by about eighteen months.

It is also, usefully for this site, dollarized. El Salvador retired the colón in 2001 and has used the US dollar ever since. The prices below are therefore real shop prices, not a conversion through a second exchange rate. That puts the Salvadoran basket on exactly the same footing as the American one.

What actually happened to the Bitcoin Law

The 2021 law did three things: it made Bitcoin legal tender alongside the dollar, it required businesses to accept it, and it launched a state wallet called Chivo with a $30 sign-up bonus. For three and a half years that was the headline every article led with.

On 29 January 2025 the Legislative Assembly amended it. Bitcoin is no longer legal tender. Merchant acceptance is now voluntary rather than required. Taxes can no longer be paid in bitcoin. The Chivo wallet is being wound down. All of this was a condition of a $1.4 billion IMF programme, approved by the Fund’s board on 26 February 2025.

One point remains genuinely disputed and is worth stating as disputed rather than resolved. The government’s Bitcoin Office says it still buys roughly one BTC per day, and public trackers put the state’s holdings at around 7,751 BTC as of 22 August 2026. The IMF said in July 2025 that public-sector holdings had not actually grown, and that the apparent increases reflected coins being consolidated across government wallets rather than new purchases. Both claims are on the record. Pick whichever you like, but do not pretend the other one does not exist.

The adoption numbers nobody quotes

The Universidad Centroamericana’s public opinion institute has surveyed Salvadoran bitcoin use every year since the law passed. The trend is one direction only: 25.7% of respondents used bitcoin in 2021, 21% in 2022, 12% in 2023, and 8.1% in the December 2024 wave, which sampled 1,266 people nationwide.

That is the single most important fact about El Salvador’s experiment, and it is almost never the fact people lead with. Making something legal tender does not make people use it. Handing out a $30 bonus gets you a spike, not a habit. If you want to argue that Bitcoin succeeds as everyday money, El Salvador is the closest thing to a controlled experiment that exists, and the result was not encouraging.

El Zonte, where it actually started

The interesting part of the story predates the law. Bitcoin adoption in El Salvador began in the surf town of El Zonte, where an anonymous donor started funding local nonprofits in bitcoin and organisers built a small circular economy around it. President Bukele cited that project as the inspiration for the national law. Property prices in El Zonte rose 134.8% between 2015 and 2024.

The two grand projects announced alongside the law have moved slowly. The volcano bonds, delayed for years, were issued in late 2024. Bitcoin City, the geothermal-powered metropolis in the renderings, is still a special economic zone with infrastructure being laid rather than a city.

Hours of work, not just sats

The sats figure tells you about Bitcoin. It does not tell you whether life in El Salvador is affordable. For that, the useful denominator is time.

The commerce and services minimum wage is $408.80 a month as of January 2026, which works out to roughly $2.36 an hour on a standard full-time month. Agricultural workers are on $272.72. The eight-item basket above totals about $27.60, so a minimum-wage worker in the commerce sector spends something close to a day and a half of labour on it.

Run both calculations and you learn two different things. Sats per basket tells you about the monetary side; hours per basket tells you about the living-standards side. Confusing the two is the most common mistake in this entire genre of analysis, and it is usually made on purpose.

The honest caveat. Comparing a depreciating currency against Bitcoin over a period when Bitcoin rose will always favour Bitcoin. Bitcoin has also fallen more than 70% from its high on several occasions, and during those stretches it was the worse place to have been. Sources and method

How the satoshi figures are calculated. Nothing in the satoshi column is typed in by hand. Each one is the local price divided by Bitcoin’s current price in US dollars and multiplied by 100,000,000, because one bitcoin is one hundred million satoshis — the smallest unit defined in the Bitcoin white paper. The exchange rate is fetched from Blockchain.info and refreshed every sixty seconds, with Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp as fallbacks. If your browser cannot reach any of them, the figures you see are the ones calculated on 3 September 2026, when one bitcoin was $77,772.39.

Where the local prices come from. The prices in the basket are indicative national averages reviewed on 3 September 2026, not one supermarket’s shelf on one day. They vary widely by city, by shop and by season, and they are cross-checked against Numbeo's cost-of-living data for El Salvador, which is crowd-sourced and carries that same caveat. The calculator above exists precisely so you can replace them with the prices you actually pay.

Where the currency and inflation context comes from. Statements on this page about inflation, devaluation and the direction of the local currency are checked against World Bank and IMF consumer-price data for El Salvador. Figures about Bitcoin adoption are order-of-magnitude estimates from published survey and chain-analysis work, and are described as such in the text rather than quoted as precise percentages.

A note on the currency used here. El Salvador has used the US dollar as its currency since 2001, so the dollar prices here are the actual shelf prices, not a conversion.

Frequently asked questions

How many satoshis is a Salvadoran grocery basket?

The eight-item basket on this page totals about $27.60. Because El Salvador uses the US dollar, that is the actual shop price rather than a conversion, and the live table above turns it into satoshis at the current Bitcoin price.

Is Bitcoin still legal tender in El Salvador?

No. The Legislative Assembly amended the Bitcoin Law on 29 January 2025 as a condition of a $1.4 billion IMF programme. Bitcoin is no longer legal tender, merchant acceptance is voluntary, and taxes can no longer be paid in bitcoin.

How many Salvadorans actually use Bitcoin?

A December 2024 survey by the Universidad Centroamericana found 8.1% had used bitcoin that year, down from 25.7% in 2021, 21% in 2022 and 12% in 2023.

Does El Salvador still buy Bitcoin every day?

That is disputed. The government's Bitcoin Office says it still buys roughly one BTC a day. The IMF stated in July 2025 that public-sector holdings had not grown and that apparent increases reflected coins being moved between government wallets.
